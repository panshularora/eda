1Executive summary

  • Recovered the exact dataset. Following the recovery-log clue to node_07 through the recovery shell yielded Social_Engine_Posts_Corrupted.csv (12,360 × 8) and Social_Engine_Users.csv (1,500 × 5). SHA-256 hashes are pinned in the pipeline, so any altered input is detected.
  • Found nine corruption types, including four that are easy to miss: timestamps that lost their time of day (3,526 posts), two different spellings of missing values, five kinds of injected text suffixes (&amp;, <br>, <div>, Ã©, trailing newlines), and NULL tokens hidden behind those suffixes.
  • Cleaned without inventing data. Values are corrected only where the original is deterministically recoverable. Unrecoverable values stay NULL, since they are missing completely at random (every χ² test p ≥ 0.145). Imputation would add false spikes and bias Phase 2 correlations.
  • Avoided the “midnight spike” trap. Padding date-only timestamps with 00:00:00 would put 32.5% of all posts at midnight (11.1× any other hour). Real posting activity is flat across the day (p = 0.475).
  • What the data says: volume is stationary (~33 posts/day), engagement metrics are independent and uniform, no platform has a real engagement edge, followers do not predict engagement (Spearman ρ = 0.011), totals simply track post count (Spearman ρ = 0.841), user language is unrelated to location, and seasonal campaigns are not mentioned in season. Each claim is backed by a test in Section 7.

2Recovering the dataset

The rulebook withholds the dataset; it had to be recovered from the corrupted Social Engine site. Our path:

  1. The landing terminal boots and reports data_intake.service … FAILED. Enter Recovery Mode asks for a locally stored operator identity.
  2. All ten dashboard modules fail in different ways (hang, signal lost, page error, access denied, no response). The ARCHIVE module shows ARCH_LINKLOST_07. After a few failures the site hints: “Nothing responding? There may be another way in.”
  3. Each system log line begins with a timestamp and the component reporting it. Only one component is healthy: node_07 responded 200 (intermittent), then watchdog last known good node: node_07 and dashboard link to node_07: severed.
  4. The recovery shell (terminal button) accepts help, status, scan, logs. scan confirms “node_07 responding intermittently, outside normal routing. Manual reconnection may be possible.”
  5. connect node_07 stabilises the link and opens ARCHIVE NODE 07. We recovered both files listed under Dataset 01.

Integrity note. The site builds both CSVs in the browser and downloads them as Blobs, so they never show up as separate requests in the Network tab. We confirmed our downloads match the copies embedded in the site bundle byte-for-byte. SHA-256: posts 49a460d6b6d8b7f5…, users d30efe470a4dd561…. A .gitattributes rule keeps git from rewriting their line endings, so the hashes still match after a clone on any OS.

5Decisions, assumptions & alternatives

5.1 Date-only timestamps keep their date and get no invented time

3,526 posts (29.4%) carry only a date. The obvious conversion (pd.to_datetime → 2024-09-25 00:00:00) invents a time. The cleaned table therefore has post_date (always present), post_datetime_utc (NULL when unknown), timestamp_precision and timestamp_source_format.

5.2 Negative likes are sign flips; missing likes stay NULL

Two independent pieces of evidence show a sign-flip corruption rather than “dislikes”. First, the format: every negative value is a float string while every other value is an integer. Second, the distribution: the absolute values of negatives match positives (KS p = 0.626; means 2,460.7 vs 2,493.5). Missing likes cannot be recovered. They correlate with nothing, so any model reduces to the median, and median imputation would put 1,817 posts on one value (2,498) and shrink the standard deviation by 7.9%.

5.3 Missing values are missing completely at random

We tested each corruption flag against platform, timestamp source format and month (17 χ² tests). None is significant; the smallest p-value is 0.145. Missingness in one column does not predict missingness in another: likes and platform are both missing in 260 posts vs 270 expected under independence, likes and text in 282 vs 269. Because the data is MCAR, analyses that simply skip missing values are unbiased, and filling values in would add nothing but false precision.

7Exploratory analysis

Conventions: we report the test and p-value for every pattern. p ≥ 0.05 is reported as “no evidence”, never as a finding. Likes-based statistics use the 10,186 posts with known likes. Hour-of-day uses the 8,474 posts with a real time.

7.1 Volume over time

Volume is flat at 32.9 posts/day (range 18–54). Variance/mean is 1.07, i.e. Poisson noise around a constant rate. The largest month (2024-05, 1,038) and smallest (2025-02, 914) differ only by calendar length. Per-day rates run 32.1–34.2, and a χ² test of monthly totals against days-in-month gives p = 0.986. Day of week is also flat (p = 0.722). No single day is anomalous after correcting for 365 tests (Poisson tail, α = 0.05/365). Only 2 days exceed z = 3, against 0.74 expected by chance.

7.2 Engagement distributions

Likes, shares and comments are each uniform on [0, 5,000], [0, 2,000] and [0, 1,000] (KS vs uniform: p = 0.407, p = 0.173, p = 0.195), and mutually independent (Spearman ρ: likes–shares -0.001, likes–comments 0.01, shares–comments 0.024). The means (2,491.9 / 1,007.2 / 504.3) sit at the midpoints, so expected engagement splits about 5 : 2 : 1. There is no heavy tail and no viral outlier: the maxima are hard caps.

7.3 Platforms

Platform volumes are balanced (Facebook 2,074, YouTube 2,073, Twitter 2,049, Reddit 2,031, Instagram 1,989; largest vs smallest 4.3%, χ² vs uniform p = 0.658). Mean likes differ by at most 3.7% of the overall mean (p = 0.371) and comments by 2.3% (p = 0.796). Shares are the only nominal difference: Instagram 1,040.8 vs Facebook 984.2, p = 0.035. That does not survive a Bonferroni correction for four metrics (α = 0.0125), and the effect size is negligible (ε² = 0.001).

7.4 Users: activity, reach and engagement

Posts per user follow a Poisson(λ = 8.0) distribution (variance/mean 1.02, range 1–22). 1,479 users (98.6%) post on 2+ platforms, averaging 3.73. A null model that assigns each post a random platform predicts 1,475 and 3.71, so cross-platform posting is not a behaviour, just chance. Followers (uniform 109–49,944, KS p = 0.870) do not predict engagement per post (ρ = 0.011, p = 0.666) or activity (ρ = -0.01).

Ranking users by total engagement mostly ranks post counts (ρ = 0.841). The top 10 by total average 15.9 posts, twice the norm, and none of them appears in the top 10 by mean engagement per post (minimum 5 posts with known likes). The per-post leaders are:

7.5 Language and location

The ten languages are equally common (p = 0.895 vs uniform) and statistically independent of country (χ² p = 0.419, Cramér's V = 0.111). Only 12.8% of users speak a national language of their country, against 12.4% expected from random assignment (binomial p = 0.667). Examples: Berlin, Germany → ja, Munich, Germany → zh, São Paulo, Brazil → ar. The 132 users in Italy and South Korea cannot match at all, since it and ko are absent. The most represented country is the USA (203 users).

7.6 Text content

The 10,221 texts are near-unique (10,219 distinct) but built from templates: six openers such as “Just unboxed my new…”, “Just saw an ad for…” and “Comparing…” each start 980–1,052 posts. Mentions spread evenly over 10 brands (977–1,070 posts each), 29 hashtags (2.01 per post) and 15 @handles (in 13.5% of posts). Verdict phrases split into positive 3,268, negative 3,306 and neutral 1,581. Engagement does not differ by verdict (p = 0.221). 40.9% of posts that state an emotion contradict their own verdict, e.g. “Bummed out with my new Air Max from Nike! Absolutely loving it. #Travel, #CustomerService, #Fashion”. Seasonal campaigns land in their season 14.5% of the time vs 15.0% by chance; SpringBlast2025 even appears in 96 posts dated 2024.

8Key insights

1 · The corruption is machine-injected at fixed rates, uniformly at random data quality

~15% missing in each of platform, likes and text; 13.41% text suffixes (five kinds of ~2.7% each); 4.24% sign flips; 2.93% duplicates. All 17 dependence tests are non-significant, co-missingness matches independence, and each missing column mixes '' and NULL at the same ~2:1 ratio. That points to a single random corruption process, not a failing source.

So what / Phase 2: Available-case SQL (WHERE likes IS NOT NULL) is unbiased. Keep NULLs; do not COALESCE to a median.

2 · The “midnight posting spike” is an artifact of bad parsing anomaly

29.4% of timestamps have no time of day. Defaulting them to 00:00 makes hour 0 hold 3,902 posts (11.1× any other hour). With real times only, posting is flat across all 24 hours (311–388 per hour, p = 0.475).

So what / Phase 2: Any hour-of-day query must filter timestamp_precision = 'second'; day, week and month queries can use post_date for all rows.

3 · Activity is stationary: no trend, seasonality or anomalous day trend

32.9 posts/day with Poisson noise. Monthly totals are explained by days-in-month (p = 0.986), weekdays are flat (p = 0.722), and no day survives a Bonferroni-corrected Poisson test. The “May peak” and “February dip” are 31-day and 28-day months.

So what / Phase 2: Normalise counts by days (posts/day) before comparing periods, and test spikes against Poisson noise before calling them anomalies.

4 · Likes, shares and comments are independent uniform variables correlation

Uniform on fixed caps (KS p ≥ 0.17), |ρ| ≤ 0.024, no long tail. Real social data is heavy-tailed and positively correlated, so this looks like synthetic generation. A post with many likes is no more likely to be shared.

So what / Phase 2: Expect near-zero correlations. Define “high engagement” by percentile (e.g. top 10%) rather than absolute thresholds, and use rank-based statistics.

5 · No platform has a real engagement advantage grouping

Gaps are ≤ 5.6% of the mean. The one nominal result (shares, Instagram vs Facebook, p = 0.035) fails multiple-testing correction, with ε² = 0.001.

So what / Phase 2: Platform-level rankings will be driven by noise. Report confidence intervals alongside any GROUP BY platform result.

6 · Volume, not reach, drives total engagement behaviour

Followers show no relationship to per-post engagement (ρ = 0.011). Total engagement tracks post count (ρ = 0.841), and the top-10 by total and top-10 by per-post average share zero users. Cross-platform posting (98.6% of users) matches a random-assignment null model.

So what / Phase 2: For behavioural grouping, rank by per-post rates with a minimum-activity threshold (window functions such as NTILE/PERCENT_RANK), not by SUM(). “Influencer” cannot be inferred from follower_count.

7 · User language is not a location signal consistency

Language is independent of country (V = 0.111), with a national-language match of 12.8% vs 12.4% by chance. 132 users live in countries whose language is not coded at all.

So what / Phase 2: Treat language and country as separate dimensions. Do not derive regional language claims (e.g. “Asia-Pacific language dominance”) from this field.

8 · Post text is templated and carries no temporal or engagement signal text

Brands, hashtags and verdicts are evenly balanced. 40.9% of emotional posts contradict their own verdict, engagement does not vary by sentiment (p = 0.221), and campaigns are mentioned out of season (e.g. SpringBlast2025 in 2024).

So what / Phase 2: Text features (brand, campaign, sentiment) suit descriptive counts only. Do not expect them to explain engagement or timing.

9Limitations & Phase 2 readiness

  • Unrecoverable values remain NULL: platform 14.9%, likes 15.1%, text 14.8%, time of day 29.4%. This is a deliberate trade of completeness for correctness.
  • Timezone: A1 cannot be proven for naive ISO strings. Hour-of-day findings would shift by a constant if the source clock were not UTC.
  • Sentiment relies on the dataset's fixed verdict phrases, not a general NLP model. It is exact for these templates but not transferable.
  • SQL-ready: the cleaned CSVs load directly into a two-table schema (users 1—N posts) with typed columns, primary and foreign keys, and CHECK constraints that mirror our validation (sql/schema.sql, loaded by src/build_sqlite.py).
